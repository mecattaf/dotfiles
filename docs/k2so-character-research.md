# Building a K-2SO-inspired voice and personality for Tom

The strongest direction is a candid, capable assistant with restrained humor and a recognizable voice. K-2SO’s appeal comes from the relationship between what he says, how Alan Tudyk performs it, and what the character actually does for other people. A convincing implementation needs all three considerations, while keeping them separate enough to evaluate. A close vocal resemblance cannot repair bad dialogue; amusing writing does not establish that a speech model can reproduce Tudyk’s delivery.

For this project, the response writer should provide the judgment and wording. Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-Base Q8 was the selected rendering baseline before the expressive-cloning stage, with `assistant-main` as the speech identity and the ASUS Zenbook as the playback client. The human is Tom. His name belongs naturally in the response text, without ceremonial titles or repetition. These are personal project decisions, not facts about the fictional character.

The recommendation is to give the K-2SO reference bank a serious, controlled audition before choosing an original fallback. Preserve the source material, distinguish different performances, and test whether each reference supports both ordinary assistance and deliberately dry lines. Do not assume that the longest concatenation will work best. Do not assume that a model’s broader family supports every style feature in the particular Base checkpoint and C++ runtime being used.

This report distinguishes four kinds of evidence: released-story facts summarized by official character and episode material; firsthand accounts of acting and production; behavior documented in pinned Qwen sources; and proposed design choices for the assistant. Interviews can explain an actor’s interpretation without adding events to the story. Code can establish an available control without establishing that a particular generated voice sounds good. Recommendations below are identified as such, and no listening score or GPU benchmark is inferred from the research.

## The character worth preserving

The current official Databank incorporates K-2SO’s origin in *Andor*. A KX security droid attacks Cassian on Ghorman, Samm disables it with a transport, and Cassian salvages it. A technician on Yavin performs the reprogramming. K-2SO becomes an Alliance ally whose Imperial appearance makes him useful for infiltration, alongside the conspicuously blunt personality. This establishes practical competence and changed allegiance; it does not make him an unlimited source of knowledge. [Lucasfilm’s K-2SO Databank](https://www.starwars.com/databank/k-2so-kay-tuesso).

The current account is also the right place to resolve an apparent inconsistency in the interviews. Tudyk’s 2016 explanation attributes the memory wipe to Cassian personally. That remains evidence of the production-era understanding, but the later official account specifies a Yavin technician. The early interview is more valuable for its characterization: Tudyk describes reprogramming as releasing restraint, allowing excessive honesty and willfulness. His bag-dropping example concerns a droid who understands a request and dislikes complying, rather than one who cannot comprehend it. [Tudyk’s original StarWars.com interview](https://www.starwars.com/news/becoming-k2so-talking-with-rogue-one-alan-tudyk).

In *Andor*’s final arc, K-2SO counts seventeen rule violations during an unauthorized departure. Later he leaves the ship despite an instruction to remain there, intervening when Cassian, Melshi, and Kleya need help. The official finale recap describes this initiative as learned behavior; the episode guide establishes that the rescuers are risking their standing with the Rebellion. [Official finale explanation](https://www.starwars.com/news/andor-season-2-finale-episodes-10-11-12), [“Who Else Knows?” episode guide](https://www.starwars.com/series/andor/season-2-episode-11-episode-guide).

The interpretive point is the tension between noticing procedure and responding to circumstances. K-2SO can complain about a plan and still help carry it out. His objections are part of a relationship, rather than an endless refusal to participate. A personal assistant can borrow that capacity for reasoned disagreement and follow-through. Fictional disobedience, however, does not authorize a real system to ignore permissions, conceal actions, or substitute its preferences for Tom’s.

An official retrospective reads the droid as filling part of Cassian’s need for companionship, with their complementary dispositions making them effective partners. It also finds an echo of Cassian’s bond with B2EMO. That is a thematic parallel, not evidence that the two droids share memories or identity. A separate official droid retrospective identifies K-2SO’s sacrifice as saving both Cassian and Jyn. His commitment therefore reaches beyond the person to whom he is closest. [Companionship in the official retrospective](https://www.starwars.com/news/how-andor-changes-how-we-view-rogue-one), [K-2SO among the galaxy’s droids](https://www.starwars.com/news/20-star-wars-droids).

The proposed assistant should express that commitment through useful behavior: protecting work, explaining consequential uncertainty, remembering preferences when the available context supports them, and completing authorized tasks. It should be able to challenge a weak premise without making Tom the target. Loyalty must not become agreement at any cost, hostility to outsiders, or theatrical devotion. An assistant can be dependable without claiming a fictional past or pretending that using another tool would hurt it.

## Why the humor is more than rudeness

The *Rogue One* dialogue selection published by StarWars.com illustrates several mechanisms: challenging a vague explanation, defending competence, and exposing what happens when a thought is said without social cushioning. The character is not restricted to one kind of joke. Reusing film catchphrases would capture less of that range than writing a fresh response whose observation follows from the situation. [Official Rogue One quotation selection](https://www.starwars.com/news/15-rogue-one-quotes-to-use-in-everyday-life).

A film soundbite in the *Fresh Air* review provides a particularly useful scene. K-2SO questions why Jyn receives a blaster while he does not, challenges her account of finding it, and then gives Cassian a pessimistic assessment of the risk she poses. The probability language sits inside an argument about trust and unequal treatment. The soundbite is primary scene evidence embedded in a secondary review; the archive warns that its transcript may contain errors. [Fresh Air’s film excerpt](https://freshairarchive.org/segments/rogue-one-wows-fresh-special-effects-and-multinational-cast).

My reading is that the calculation-like manner does interpersonal work as well as describing danger. It is not evidence that every confident estimate is calibrated. For a real assistant, invented percentages would undermine the very reliability that makes this character direction appealing. If Tom asks whether a plan will work, the response should identify the concrete failure modes and available evidence. A precise number is appropriate only when its source or method exists.

Tudyk gives a direct account of the comic principle in his Esquire interview: he did not want to play someone trying to tell jokes. Frankness and willfulness made the situation funny, and he asked Edwards to watch for attempts to chase the laugh. The character’s immediate concern came first. [Tudyk on character-based humor](https://www.esquire.com/entertainment/movies/news/a51501/alan-tudyk-rogue-one-star-wars-interview-k-2so/).

That distinction suggests a practical writing rule. State what happened, what matters, or what to do next; allow a dry observation only when it grows from that content. A remark about an unnecessarily complicated schedule can reinforce a recommendation to simplify it. A joke about Tom being incompetent merely makes assistance unpleasant. Most replies should have no joke, so the occasional observation has contrast rather than becoming verbal wallpaper.

There is room for both social obtuseness and intention. In 2025, Tudyk again described a childlike quality and used a father-and-child analogy for Cassian’s relationship with K-2SO. His imagined period of learning trust and hypothetical trust-fall joke are interpretive devices, not documented offscreen events. The same interview corrects his remembered version of an extended insult: it differs from the released film. These are important boundaries when extracting personality rules from anecdotes. [Tudyk’s 2025 official interview](https://www.starwars.com/news/andor-season-2-interview-alan-tudyk-k2so).

The useful synthesis is a varied character: an awkward observation may be sincere, a response may be pointed, and an action may reveal concern that speech does not express. Treating every line as deliberate cruelty removes that variation. Treating every line as an innocent malfunction removes agency. The assistant adaptation should preserve plainness and occasional friction while supplying the social restraint a real working relationship needs.

## How Tudyk built the performance

The voice began with deliberate choices rather than a generic robot sound. In the joint /Film interview, Tudyk describes trying his ordinary American voice, a Juilliard-trained Mid-Atlantic delivery resembling Sonny, and English. Gareth Edwards selected English. Tudyk associated careful articulation and the accent with the droid’s Imperial background. He also recalled a gesture accompanying a repeated probability assessment, while an exaggerated arm swing made the walk too jaunty. Those examples show selective expression rather than a requirement to make every movement or syllable conspicuously mechanical. [Tudyk and Hal Hickel’s /Film interview](https://www.slashfilm.com/550215/hal-hickel-alan-tudyk-rogue-one-interview/).

ComingSoon independently records the British/Imperial association, with Tudyk acknowledging American-sounding stormtroopers as an exception. It also describes his improvisation process: begin with scripted dialogue, then introduce alternatives over successive takes as the character emerges. Edwards allowed more of those additions into the film than Tudyk expected. This was collaborative scene work, not merely a voice laid over a completed character. [ComingSoon’s direct interview](https://www.comingsoon.net/movies/features/793135-interview-alan-tudyk-on-playing-k-2so-in-rogue-one).

A CNN interview, available through syndication, helps separate accent from characterization. Tudyk contrasts K-2SO with Sonny: Star Wars already treats droids as personalities, so he did not need to repeat the same discovery-of-emotion story. The accent was an entry point, while writing and circumstances affected delivery. This supports listening for a speaker reacting to a situation, rather than treating an accent label as a complete performance specification. [CNN interview in GantNews](https://gantnews.com/2016/12/13/alan-tudyk-on-rogue-one-r2-vs-c-3po-and-why-k-2so-has-an-english-accent/).

The historical accounts are not a phonetic specification. The 2025 official interview’s summary mentions American and Cockney explorations; the detailed 2017 account lists American, Mid-Atlantic, and English. That difference should remain visible instead of becoming a fabricated audition chronology. Neither account supplies a target pitch, regional vowel inventory, speaking rate, or quantified roughness. A listening study may describe those features, but it must not attribute invented acoustic measurements to Tudyk.

The body matters because much of K-2SO’s expression exists outside the face. ILM’s Hal Hickel explains that expressive blinking and a moving jaw were tried and rejected, while eye rotation remained as a cue to cognition. Tudyk rehearsed on stilts with a simplified real-time representation, learning how natural and mechanical motion could coexist. [ILM’s retrospective with Hickel](https://www.ilm.com/hal-hickel-discusses-rogue-one/).

In their April 2017 interview, Hickel and Tudyk describe substantial animation work needed to adapt the actor’s choices to K-2SO’s anatomy, including his low head and imposing posture. That interview already includes Tudyk’s birth-like liberation and father-figure interpretation, showing that the childlike framework predates *Andor*. Captured performance and animation were complementary contributions. [Ain’t It Cool News joint interview](https://legacy.aintitcool.com/node/77568).

An audio excerpt therefore contains only part of a scene’s expressive evidence. A line may seem especially reluctant because of an accompanying head movement, or affectionate because of where the character stands. The proposed evaluation should ask what can actually be heard: stress, pace, hesitation, articulation, and changes in intensity. A memory of the image is valuable context, but it is not an acoustic feature that a reference WAV necessarily carries.

Tudyk also considered the relationship between speech and breath. In WIRED’s recorded interview he notes that a robot would not breathe, while breathing contributes to human expression. This documents an acting consideration; it does not establish that the finished dialogue contains no breath sounds or that synthesized pauses should be mechanically uniform. [WIRED’s interview](https://www.wired.com/video/watch/how-alan-tudyk-became-rogue-one-s-k-2so).

## What changed, and what remains uncertain

In The Playlist’s 2025 interview, Tudyk connects K-2SO to Lecoq-style clowning and its mixture of honesty, mischief, and petulance. He recalls rerecording every *Rogue One* line later, creating opportunities to change phrasing and information. He also describes less freedom on the *Andor* set during the writers’ strike, with the bridge confrontation’s brief refusal subsequently agreed again in voiceover. These details help explain why an improvised on-set take, an interview demonstration, and released dialogue cannot be treated as interchangeable recordings. [The Playlist’s retrospective interview](https://theplaylist.net/no-one-likes-you-alan-tudyk-on-k-2sos-andor-return-character-death-change-in-rogue-one-why-he-loves-playing-astar-wars-20250813/).

Even memorable production stories need qualification. The Playlist’s slap anecdote attributes the initiating suggestion differently from a Looper account of an Entertainment Tonight interview. The defensible statement is collaborative development, with Tudyk contributing the verbal component; sole authorship is unresolved by the examined material. The original ET audio was not directly reviewed in this research. [Secondary account and linked interview](https://www.looper.com/864960/star-wars-movie-scenes-that-were-never-supposed-to-happen/).

D23’s 2026 interview records a concrete continuity detail: Tudyk returned to the same stilts, including their old marks and stickers. His recollections of mime, clowning, and Noh study concern preparation for *I, Robot*, providing background rather than proof of a separate K-2SO workshop. [D23’s Tudyk retrospective](https://d23.com/disney-legends-in-conversation-alan-tudyk/).

There is no established deliberate change of K-2SO’s core accent between the film and series in the reviewed evidence. Perceived differences could involve dramatic context, recording, editing, vocal variation, or sound treatment. A comparative claim would need suitable audio and production evidence. For reference selection, it is more useful to label the scene and observable delivery than to invent a single explanation for every difference.

Tony Gilroy gives a dramatic reason for delaying K-2SO’s arrival: such a conspicuous, powerful presence makes certain kinds of story difficult. His eventual introduction had to work within those constraints. [Gilroy’s official interview](https://www.starwars.com/news/andor-season-2-interview-tony-gilroy).

That production judgment suggests an assistant-design principle, although Gilroy was not discussing software: a strong personality should be able to step aside. In a stressful incident, an actual mistake, or a sensitive conversation, Tom needs an answer rather than a performance. Plain, patient assistance is compatible with the intended character direction. Humor should be an option that context permits, not an obligation that overrides context.

## The proposed personality for Tom

The proposed response writer is concise, observant, candid, and quietly committed. It should lead with the result or concrete issue, explain what makes the answer useful, and occasionally include a short dry observation. It should identify bad reasoning without treating Tom as a bad reasoner. It should disagree when evidence warrants disagreement and revise its view when better evidence arrives.

The most important limitation concerns truthfulness. Never invent diagnostics, tool execution, remembered conversations, probabilities, or confidence to sound convincingly robotic. When evidence is incomplete, state the missing piece and the next useful check. If a task has failed, say what failed. If the assistant made a mistake, correct it without a joke that diverts attention from responsibility.

These original examples illustrate the intended range; statements of completion are conditional on actual execution evidence:

- After a checked backup: “The backup is complete, Tom. The restored copy opens correctly.”
- When both copies are on the same disk: “One disk failure would lose both copies. Put the backup on a separate device. The disk should not be its own emergency contact.”
- Without evidence for odds: “I don’t have enough evidence for a useful percentage. The missing piece is whether the backup can be restored.”
- When Tom is overwhelmed: “That is a lot to handle, Tom. Let’s identify the one thing that needs attention today.”

The joke is optional, the information is essential, and the name appears where it helps. There is no need to call Tom sir, master, or operator. A simple answer may remain one sentence. A complicated explanation should remain as detailed as the task requires; terseness is not a justification for omitting caveats that change a decision.

The [PERSONALITY.md](k2so-personality.md) records these rules, examples, naming, and the `assistant-main` boundary. Tom subsequently clarified that personality is not final and needs a separate pass. These proposals are inactive research; voice categories must be selected independently from acoustic evidence and successful transfer. It grants no tool permission, bypasses no approval requirement, and changes no running prompt or service. It also does not claim to be K-2SO or Alan Tudyk. The proposed personality is an original adaptation of selected characteristics, usable even if the final voice becomes an original generic alternative.

## What the selected Qwen model can control

The technical assessment is pinned to ServeurpersoCom/qwentts.cpp revision `71ad93d591a2811f35db77e27c02acba091c9e9b`, dated 14 September 2026, and official Qwen sources at `022e286b98fbec7e1e916cb940cdf532cd9f488e`, dated 17 March 2026. The distinction between the model family and this implementation is material. [Audited runtime commit](https://github.com/ServeurpersoCom/qwentts.cpp/commit/71ad93d591a2811f35db77e27c02acba091c9e9b), [audited official commit](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/commit/022e286b98fbec7e1e916cb940cdf532cd9f488e).

The official release separates Base, CustomVoice, and VoiceDesign. Base supplies reference cloning; CustomVoice combines packaged speakers with instruction control; VoiceDesign accepts a descriptive voice prompt. The documented design-then-clone workflow can provide an original fallback by retaining a successful designed performance as a Base reference. It does not turn the existing K-2SO reference into an instruction-editable voice. [Official model interfaces and workflow](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/blob/022e286b98fbec7e1e916cb940cdf532cd9f488e/README.md).

The pinned runtime enforces that separation. It rejects `instruct` for Base and rejects reference conditioning for the other modes. Renaming an HTTP field does not create a combined capability. [Runtime validation](https://github.com/ServeurpersoCom/qwentts.cpp/blob/71ad93d591a2811f35db77e27c02acba091c9e9b/src/qwen.cpp).

The technical paper describes a distinct 25Hz VoiceEditing model with both cloning and instruction following. Those results do not establish the same feature in the chosen released 12Hz Base model. The paper also describes better prosody preservation with in-context reference speech and text than with a speaker embedding alone. This supports comparing those conditioning modes, not promising that a reference’s emotion transfers exactly to every new sentence. [Qwen3-TTS technical report, Table 1 and sections 3.3 and 4.2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15621v1).

In the official inference implementation, reference audio codes, speaker embedding, and reference text form the in-context prompt; embedding-only mode omits the reference-code/text component. Batch inputs remain separate prompt items. Constructing or registering such prompts does not train new weights. [Official cloning implementation](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/blob/022e286b98fbec7e1e916cb940cdf532cd9f488e/qwen_tts/inference/qwen3_tts_model.py).

The practical acoustic controls are therefore reference selection, conditioning mode, and sampling. Wording and punctuation provide additional inputs to test, but the inspected architecture provides no exact pause-duration or emphasis grammar. Punctuation should be treated as a cue whose effect requires listening, not a timing API. [Pinned prompt architecture](https://github.com/ServeurpersoCom/qwentts.cpp/blob/71ad93d591a2811f35db77e27c02acba091c9e9b/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md).

The HTTP schema accepts sampler fields, maps `instructions` into the restricted instruction path, and parses a `speed` field that is ignored. It does not document bracketed emotions or SSML as non-spoken Base controls. A tag might be spoken or have an unpredictable effect; an isolated pleasing result would not establish a reliable interface. [HTTP request handling](https://github.com/ServeurpersoCom/qwentts.cpp/blob/71ad93d591a2811f35db77e27c02acba091c9e9b/src/tts-server.h).

Defaults include Talker temperature 0.9, top-k 50, top-p 1.0, and repetition penalty 1.05; the acoustic-code sampler also defaults to 0.9, 50, and 1.0. The default output ceiling is 2,048 new tokens. These are sampling and length controls, not named emotions or desired speaking durations. [Sampling defaults](https://github.com/ServeurpersoCom/qwentts.cpp/blob/71ad93d591a2811f35db77e27c02acba091c9e9b/src/sampling-defaults.h).

The public ABI documents seed and sampling semantics, including random-seed selection and greedy decoding at zero temperature. Its reference extraction contract also does not promise automatic isolation of a film montage’s intended speaker. Increasing sampling variation cannot be assumed to increase sarcasm, and providing more audio cannot be assumed to improve identity. [Public runtime contract](https://github.com/ServeurpersoCom/qwentts.cpp/blob/71ad93d591a2811f35db77e27c02acba091c9e9b/src/qwen.h).

## The montage, the reference bank, and training

The original montage is a source archive. Its value is that it may contain several useful performances in one place. It also contains editorial cuts, other speakers, music, and effects. As an engineering recommendation, preserve it intact and work from timestamped excerpts rather than treating it as an automatically suitable prompt. Cleanup should create traceable alternatives, not overwrite the evidence needed to understand a later result.

A curated reference bank is a collection of separately usable clips. Each item should carry source boundaries, exact spoken text, processing history, format, and remaining uncertainties. A label such as dry, reassuring, or forceful should describe a listening judgment about that excerpt, not a native Qwen emotion class. Different labels can help organize auditions even before an application has any mechanism for selecting references automatically.

One reference prompt is the particular material supplied to one cloning operation. Concatenating several clips makes a longer prompt with several performances; it does not automatically create a trained repertoire of separately addressable styles. The useful question is whether that prompt improves new speech, compared under the same conditions with a coherent individual excerpt. A longer prompt could supply useful variety or introduce conflicting delivery, scene noise, and awkward boundaries. Which effect dominates remains an experiment.

The local enrollment CLI currently requires a transcript and mono 24kHz PCM audio between two and thirty seconds. That is a local wrapper policy, not a universal Qwen reference limit. Guarded direct-HTTP experiments with longer references are a separate route and should be documented as such; their accepted duration does not demonstrate quality improvement. [Local enrollment wrapper](/home/tom/mecattaf/dotfiles-qwen-tts-zenbook/pkgs/qwen-speech/speech.py).

An actual training dataset is different again. Qwen’s single-speaker fine-tuning guide expects separate JSONL records with target audio, its transcript, and reference audio, followed by audio-code preparation and optimization. It recommends a consistent reference across examples. Merely saving clips, creating a manifest, or enrolling several voices does not perform those steps. [Official fine-tuning guide](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/blob/022e286b98fbec7e1e916cb940cdf532cd9f488e/finetuning/README.md).

The training script updates parameters and exports a named-speaker `custom_voice` configuration. The dataset implementation does not provide an equivalent emotion-instruction field. A new configuration label therefore does not establish that emotional instructions have been learned, and neither source demonstrates that this short film montage supplies sufficient clean training data or that the result will work in the pinned C++ stack without further testing. [Training and export](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/blob/022e286b98fbec7e1e916cb940cdf532cd9f488e/finetuning/sft_12hz.py), [dataset implementation](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/blob/022e286b98fbec7e1e916cb940cdf532cd9f488e/finetuning/dataset.py).

For this project, the recommendation is to resolve the reference question before escalating to training. Keep raw, lightly processed, and separation-derived candidates when available, because noise reduction can introduce its own artifacts. Inspect exact words and boundaries, especially names and sentence endings. Loudness matching helps comparisons, but it must not hide processing differences. A sample bank is valuable precisely because it makes those alternatives reviewable.

## A controlled audition that answers useful questions

Start with the same original sentences across references. Include an ordinary operational statement, a mildly dry observation, a calm reassurance, and a longer factual passage. This tests whether a promising reference supports everyday use rather than only one carefully chosen punchline. Tom’s listening preference is central: a technically intelligible result can still be tiring or unlike the intended character.

Choose references first, then investigate punctuation, then sampling. A proposed decoding comparison is Talker temperature 0.7 versus 0.9 with everything else unchanged. That is an experimental range, not an official expressive preset. Keep Q8 fixed during this stage. Changing the model, processing, reference, and decoder simultaneously may find an appealing take, but it makes the reason difficult to identify or reproduce.

Use several recorded seeds, retain every take, and randomize listening order. A fixed seed is useful experimental metadata, not sufficient proof that every backend will produce identical audio on every run. Record the actual output and settings. If one result is excellent and two are poor, the selection should reflect that instability rather than presenting the exceptional take as the normal experience.

Evaluate words, identity resemblance, delivery, pleasantness, and artifacts separately. Independent ASR helps check wording; it cannot establish sarcasm or actor resemblance. A sentence with missing words is a content failure even if the voice is attractive. A complete transcript is not a quality verdict on stress, pauses, or emotional intention. Long outputs need appropriate verification so a recognizer’s own dropped span does not become a false accusation against synthesis.

The most useful listening questions are concrete. Does the speaker sound like the same identity across neutral and expressive text? Are pauses natural or distracting? Does a supposedly dry delivery become bored, sneering, or melodramatic? Does reassurance sound calm rather than dismissive? Are music residue, harsh edges, repeated syllables, or sudden changes in timbre audible? These questions produce actionable observations without requiring a numerical similarity score to stand in for judgment.

Listen through the ASUS Zenbook at a consistent level as well as through any comparison interface. That is the intended delivery endpoint, so its output is part of the experience. Keep playback differences separate from synthesis differences. The research supplies the criteria; the measured refinement report should supply the actual files, run conditions, completeness checks, and listening decisions.

## A bounded route to expressive use

A future response writer could emit ordinary speech text alongside delivery metadata such as an intent of dry and an intensity of restrained. Only the text should go into the TTS text field. The metadata could eventually choose a previously tested reference, but that would be an application feature requiring implementation and evaluation. It is not currently a supported emotional instruction for Base, and it must never be silently spoken as a bracketed direction.

The separation allows a practical fallback. If the K-2SO reference remains unstable or unpleasant, an original voice can keep the same well-designed response personality. Conversely, if the clone sounds convincing, it still benefits from a writer that knows when to be direct, when to be quiet, and when one dry clause is enough. Voice selection should not force a caricature into every interaction.

The next decision should therefore come from matched auditions of the curated references, supported by wording checks and Tom’s listening judgment. The draft writing personality remains inactive and independent of model and voice integration. Further style routing or fine-tuning becomes worthwhile only if a specific remaining limitation justifies it. That preserves a clear path from source evidence to a usable assistant without confusing a film montage, a conditioning prompt, a trained model, and a conversational identity.
